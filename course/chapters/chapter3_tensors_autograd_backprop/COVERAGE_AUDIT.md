# Coverage audit — Lecture 3 materials

*Author's self-reflection, written after the materials were drafted and reviewed. Date: 2026-09-14.*

This file answers one question: **does every bullet of the syllabus have a home in these materials, grounded in a real source, with no solutions leaked to the student?** It records where each bullet is covered, what an independent review found, what was changed in response, and what remains open. It is kept in the folder deliberately: the course teaches that a claim of coverage without evidence is worth nothing, so the materials should hold themselves to the same standard.

## Method

1. Drafted the eight lecture parts, the reading list, the build and the two drills from the syllabus.
2. Researched every bullet on the open web *before* writing — three parallel source-verification passes over ~45 URLs (PyTorch docs and notes, the AD survey, the checkpointing and activation-memory papers, ZeRO, scaling laws, micrograd, CS231n, d2l, Hugging Face, the SRE book, Python docs). Only sources that actually loaded are quoted; where a page rendered only its navigation, the same text was read from the upstream docstring or an installed PyTorch 2.14 and marked as such in `READING.md`. Sources that could not be reached at all are listed at the bottom of `READING.md`.
3. Executed every PyTorch behaviour the notes assert (error strings, `.to()` identity, leaf status after `.to()`, type promotion, `x[[0]] += 1`, the version-counter error, the meta device, the absence of an MPS peak counter) on PyTorch 2.14.0 and corrected the text where it was wrong.
4. Validated `test_value.py` against a private reference engine (not shipped): 43/43 pass. The shipped skeleton fails 42/43 cleanly with `NotImplementedError` and passes only the constructor test. Two deliberately broken variants were also run: a micrograd-style recursive traversal fails exactly `test_deep_chain_does_not_hit_recursion_limit`; an engine that does not reset interior grads fails exactly `test_repeated_backward_doubles_leaf_grads_only`.
5. Ran an independent review (fresh reader, no access to the drafting context) against the syllabus with strict criteria, a solution-leakage check, a cross-file consistency check, a factual red-flag pass (every arithmetic example re-derived; every test expectation re-derived by calculus), and a source-list completeness check. Findings and fixes are below.

## C. Solution leakage — residual risk

- **Derivative rules** for `+ × ** exp tanh relu log` are in `lecture/06` §6.2 as a table. This is lecture content (as the F1 formula was in Lecture 1) and the build's difficulty is the graph and `backward()`, not the calculus. Accepted.
- **The topological-sort idea** is described in prose in `04` §4.3 and `06` §6.3 ("depth-first … in reverse order") and the skeleton ships an iterative `graph_nodes` traversal with a visited set. Combined with the "use an explicit stack" hint in the symptom table, the student has every ingredient except the post-order twist, which is the actual difficulty (a plain DFS order is *not* topological — `test_diamond_graph_needs_correct_order` exists to prove it). No `backward()` code is given anywhere. Accepted as borderline.
- **micrograd's `engine.py`** is linked in `READING.md` with "read *after* your own `backward()` works" — honour system, as with every reference in this course. The tests are stricter than micrograd in three named places precisely so that copying it does not pass.
- **`_engine` in the tests** is a four-line statement of convention 9's `analytical_grad`. Unavoidable and trivial.

## D. Known gaps and deliberate scoping

- **Griewank & Walther's exact "cheap gradient" wording** could not be fetched (SIAM blocked); part 6 attributes the *bound* to Baydin's quotation of it and the *phrase* to the book without quoting the book. Griewank (1992) likewise is cited by title and DOI only.
- **The AWS Builders' Library articles** on load shedding and retries did not render; the system design drill leans on the SRE book chapters and Brooker's 2015 post instead, and names the two AWS pieces as further reading.
- **No MPS peak-memory counter** exists in 2.14 (`dir(torch.mps)` checked); the production drill's three-sample workaround is therefore necessary and is documented as such. If a later release adds one, the drill should be simplified.
- **Hugging Face's memory pages were rewritten** between the source's original form and 2026-09; the notes quote the current text ("~20 %", "6 bytes per parameter") rather than the older phrasing many blog posts still repeat.
- **Per-layer vs √n checkpointing** is now distinguished, but the chapter does not cover checkpoint *placement* optimization (revolve, the `O(log n)` schedule in practice) beyond naming it; that is out of scope for a fully theoretical 2 h lecture and is noted in `08` §8.2.
- **`torch.compile`, CUDA graphs, and memory-efficient attention internals** are mentioned only as "fused kernels"; FlashAttention's tiling is Lecture 11's material and is deliberately not derived here.
- The **YouTube lecture's duration** ("~2 h 25 min") could not be confirmed programmatically (rate-limited); the title and channel were confirmed via oEmbed.
